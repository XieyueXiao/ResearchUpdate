## Papers Found (2026-09-11)

### 1. Antifungal therapy improves microbiome dynamics in inflammatory bowel disease
- **Journal:** Nature Medicine (IF ~82)
- **Highlights:** Prospective observational study (IVAN: "IBD Therapy Via Antifungal-driven Microbiota Normalization") from Weill Cornell Medicine enrolling 53 patients with mild-to-moderate Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis who also had mild oral thrush, testing whether targeting intestinal fungal dysbiosis (Candida overgrowth) with the antifungal fluconazole can reshape gut ecology and improve disease activity. Fluconazole, but not nystatin, effectively reduced intestinal Candida burden and reshaped gut mycobiome composition; this was accompanied by increased bacterial diversity and enhanced butyrate production. Over an 8-week follow-up, fluconazole-treated patients were more likely to see reduced IBD disease activity and less likely to experience clinical worsening, providing what the authors describe as the first evidence that correcting fungal dysbiosis can favorably remodel bacterial-fungal microbiome dynamics in IBD and establishing a framework for mycobiome-guided precision therapies.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04616-y)
