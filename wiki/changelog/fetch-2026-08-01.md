## Papers Found (2026-08-01)

### 1. Gut microbiota restricts intestinal lipid uptake via modulation of bile phosphatidylcholine metabolism in mice
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology (IF ~28)
- **Highlights:** Brunner, Plagge, Zimmermann-Kogadeeva, and colleagues used lipidomics and proteomics in mice colonized with variable gut microbiota to show that the microbiota restricts intestinal lipid uptake by triggering Myd88 signalling and degrading phosphatidylcholine in bile. Bile phosphatidylcholine is a key phospholipid required to form micelles that solubilize and ferry dietary lipids to enterocytes; microbiota-driven degradation of this pool reshapes the bile lipid landscape and reduces fat absorption efficiency even at comparable dietary intake. The findings position the gut microbiota as an active regulator of the bile-mediated lipid "delivery system," adding a mechanistic layer — distinct from the goblet-cell/secondary-bile-acid axis (Liang et al., June 2026) and the mucus–metabolite axis (Holmberg & Schroeder, June 2026) already in this wiki — to how intestinal microbes shape host lipid absorption and, by extension, metabolic disease risk.
- **Link:** [DOI: 10.1038/s41564-026-02434-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02434-z)
