## Papers Found (2026-08-15)

### 1. Dietary tryptophan mitigates lung ischemia-reperfusion injury in association with increased indole-3-propionate and aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Lung ischemia-reperfusion (IR) injury drives early morbidity after lung transplantation and cardiothoracic surgery, and is known to be influenced by the gut-lung axis. Chaki et al. (UCSF Dept. of Anesthesia and Perioperative Care) tested whether a tryptophan-rich diet attenuates sterile lung IR injury in mice by boosting microbiota-derived indole metabolites and reprogramming alveolar macrophage inflammatory responses. Tryptophan-rich feeding increased circulating/pulmonary indole-3-propionate (IPA), improved post-reperfusion oxygenation, reduced lung IL-1β, and increased pulmonary Cyp1a1/Cyp1b1 expression — consistent with IPA-driven aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) activation dampening alveolar macrophage-mediated inflammation. Extends tryptophan/indole-metabolite-AhR signaling (well established in gut barrier and neuro-immune contexts) to a distal-organ (lung) IR injury model, suggesting dietary/microbiota-metabolite modulation as a preventive strategy before transplant or cardiothoracic surgery.
- **Link:** [bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.02.714281) (published online 2026-08-13 in Gut Microbes; direct tandfonline.com/DOI resolution blocked by egress proxy, consistent with prior cycles — Gut Microbes DOI not independently confirmed this cycle, flagged for human verification)
