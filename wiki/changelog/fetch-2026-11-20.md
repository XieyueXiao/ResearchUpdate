## Papers Found (2026-11-20)

### 1. Genetic toolkit for engineering an abundant but genetically intractable family of gut bacteria (exact title not retrieved)
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology (IF ~28) — journal attributed via secondary coverage only; see caveat below.
- **Highlights:** Many abundant gut taxa that produce health-relevant metabolites cannot be genetically manipulated, which blocks mechanistic and therapeutic work. A University of Chicago group reports a set of genetic tools (shuttle/expression systems) for one such family of gut bacteria. Using the toolkit, they engineered these bacteria to express an additional immune protein that supports the intestinal epithelial barrier. In mice with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), the engineered strains improved glucose regulation and reduced circulating inflammatory markers and disease severity. The work is a platform advance: it moves a previously intractable, high-abundance group into the range of engineered live biotherapeutics. Coverage is dated 2026-11-19, inside this fetch window.
- **Link:** [phys.org coverage, 2026-11-19](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-genetic-toolkit-scientists-hard-gut.html). DOI not retrieved; the journal assignment (Nature Microbiology) comes from an aggregator listing and was not confirmed against the journal site.
