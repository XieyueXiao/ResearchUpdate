## Papers Found (2026-07-26)

### 1. Fecal immunochemical tests as a proxy for gut microbiome profiling in colorectal cancer screening
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Birkeland, Kværner, Rounge et al. (CRCbiome project, University of Oslo) show that residual stool material from population-based fecal immunochemical test (FIT) colorectal cancer screening programs can be repurposed to profile the gut microbiome via shotgun metagenomics, without need for separate sample collection. Using a large screening cohort, the authors derive microbial early-detection signatures that improve discrimination of precancerous lesions (adenomas) among FIT-positive individuals beyond FIT result alone, and characterize how microbiome composition shifts across the adenoma-to-carcinoma continuum. Builds on the group's prior FIT-virome work; positions FIT-derived microbiome profiling as a scalable addition to existing CRC screening infrastructure. Published online 2026-07-23.

### 2. Probiotic Lactobacillus casei improves immune microenvironment in rheumatoid arthritis via gut microbiota-butyrate-HDAC/NF-κB signaling
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Yanmiao Ma et al. show in a rat/mouse model of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) that oral Lactobacillus casei supplementation reshapes the gut microbiota to favor increased butyrate production, which in turn inhibits HDAC activity and downstream NF-κB inflammatory signaling in immune cells, improving joint immune microenvironment and reducing arthritis severity. Links a specific probiotic-driven metabolite (butyrate) to an epigenetic/inflammatory mechanism (HDAC inhibition → NF-κB suppression) relevant to autoimmune disease beyond the gut, extending the knowledge base's gut-immune axis scope.
