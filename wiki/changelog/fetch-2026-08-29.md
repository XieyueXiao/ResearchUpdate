## Papers Found (2026-08-29)

### 1. Mouth-to-gut microbial transmission signatures enable robust, non-invasive diagnosis of gastrointestinal cancers
- **Journal:** Cell Host & Microbe (IF ~30)
- **Highlights:** Using a newly defined "mouth-to-feces" (MF) microbial transmission index, the study (co-first authors Lae-Guen Jang and Ji-Won Huh, lead contact Jihyun F. Kim, Yonsei University) shows that oral bacteria ectopically colonizing the gut are significantly enriched in patients with gastric and colorectal cancer relative to healthy controls, using paired oral/fecal shotgun metagenomics. The MF index tracked disease status and stage across independent cohorts, suggesting oral-to-gut microbial "leakage" as a generalizable, non-invasive biomarker framework for gastrointestinal cancer diagnosis and risk stratification, complementing existing oral-microbiome and fecal-microbiome single-site cancer signatures.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2026.07.007)

---

### 2. Nationwide cohort study reveals low bifidobacteria and distinct microbiota composition and function in Swedish newborns
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Eleonora Cavani et al. report a nationwide Swedish birth-cohort analysis of newborn gut microbiota, finding markedly lower Bifidobacterium abundance and a distinct overall community composition/function compared to previously published newborn cohorts from other countries, raising questions about population- and delivery/feeding-context-specific drivers of early bifidobacterial colonization relevant to infant gut-health and immune-priming research.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2026.2719244)
