### 1. Population-level characterisation of the human gut phageome and its association with microbial metabolites
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Bacteriophages are abundant in the gut but poorly catalogued at population scale, and little is known about how they relate to the metabolites that connect the microbiome to host health. Louca et al. (King's College London, with Helmholtz Munich and the University of Nottingham) analysed stool metagenomes from more than 1,000 TwinsUK participants. They modelled how viral and bacterial abundances co-vary and linked both to faecal metabolite profiles. They dereplicated the viral sequences into 116,740 non-redundant viral OTUs, and more than 99% had no close match in the largest existing gut virus catalogue. Phage communities were closely tied to bacterial community structure and also to microbial metabolites. This suggests phages may help shape the gut's metabolic output. The study is observational and the cohort is mostly older women of European ancestry, so replication in a more diverse cohort is needed. Press coverage dated 2026-11-18, so this is a catch-up item.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79230-0)

### 2. Companion study: gut bacteriophages associated with irritable bowel syndrome (exact title unconfirmed)
- **Journal:** Gut (IF ~24)
- **Highlights:** This is a companion paper from the same KCL-led team using the TwinsUK phage catalogue above. It identified 50 gut viral groups associated with IBS, most of them less abundant in people with the condition. The authors suggest these viruses may track with less favourable bacterial communities, not act as direct causes, and the design is observational. Announced with the Nature Communications paper on about 2026-11-18. The title, DOI and exact publication date could not be retrieved.
- **Link:** [KCL news release](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/viruses-identified-in-the-microbiome-which-could-influence-conditions-like-ibs) · [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1151958)

### Off-window / catch-up notes
- **Microbiome (IF ~15), about late October 2026:** Thirion et al., "Vegans have distinct profiles of their gut microbiome and multi-compartment metabolome that align with markers of metabolic health" (University of Copenhagen; Pedersen lab). It compared 75 vegans with 75 omnivorous Danes. Vegans had lower gut bacterial alpha diversity, linked to lower protein intake (12% vs 17% of energy), but more fibre-degrading taxa, better metabolic markers, and possibly a higher prevalence of *Blastocystis*. This is outside the 24 h window and was not in earlier fetches. Source: [KU news](https://news.ku.dk/all_news/2026/10/discovery-in-gut-microbiome-of-vegans-surprises-researcher). DOI not retrieved.
