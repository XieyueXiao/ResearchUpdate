## Papers Found (2026-07-29)

### 1. Butyrate epigenetically licenses sustained epithelial-T cell crosstalk for intestinal immune tolerance
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** It has been unclear how transient exposure to a gut-microbiota-derived metabolite could produce durable protection against intestinal inflammation. Northwestern/Feinberg researchers (Yingzi Cong and colleagues) show that butyrate — the short-chain fatty acid produced by fiber-fermenting gut bacteria — induces sustained transcriptional and epigenetic activation of Sat1 (spermidine/spermine N1-acetyltransferase 1) in intestinal epithelial cells (IECs), driving production of the polyamine metabolite N1-acetylspermidine. This epithelial metabolite in turn licenses IL-10 production in CD4+ T cells, sustaining epithelial–T cell crosstalk and immune tolerance well after the initiating butyrate exposure ends — i.e., a form of microbially induced epithelial "memory." In mouse models, this butyrate–Sat1–N1-acetylspermidine axis protected against inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)-like colitis. The authors propose the pathway as a mechanistic link between diet (fiber intake), microbiota-derived SCFA metabolism, and long-term maintenance of gut immune tolerance, with translational relevance for IBD once the pathway is validated in human intestinal tissue.
- **Link:** [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-75600-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75600-w)
