## Papers Found (2026-09-10)

### 1. Gut microbiota composition outperforms chronological age in predicting systemic inflammation and incident disease risk
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Population-based study analyzing paired fecal microbiota profiles, 30 plasma cytokines, and 19 biochemical/physiological measures in 1,199 adults (aged 20–72) from the DanFunD cohort, comparing microbiota composition and chronological age as predictors of systemic inflammation and subsequent disease risk. Microbiota composition explained more variance than age for 97% of cytokines and 84% of physiological variables, despite age itself associating with ~40% of inflammatory cytokines and most physiological variables. Individuals with a "Bacteroides 2" enterotype showed elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines and adverse metabolic profiles already in early adulthood — an inflammaging-like phenotype resembling people up to 37 years older — suggesting enterotype-based stratification could flag elevated disease risk well before chronological age would.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77499-9)
