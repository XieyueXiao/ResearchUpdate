## Papers Found (2026-11-19)

### 1. Gut microbiota GABA regulation by intestinal pH, osmolality and microbial consumption
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Gut bacteria make γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), a neurotransmitter linked to the gut–brain axis. It was unclear how conditions in the gut lumen control how much GABA is present. The authors tested how pH and osmolality affect GABA output by gut microbiota in colon models and in mice. Low pH and high osmolality raised microbial GABA production. The preprint ties this to changes in expression of the glutamate decarboxylase (GAD) system. Even so, physiological acidity did not raise net GABA in colon models, because other microbes consumed the GABA. In mice lacking GABA-consuming bacteria, acidification did raise GABA levels. The study suggests that net luminal GABA depends on both production and consumption by the community. This matters for psychobiotic strategies that aim to raise gut GABA. The paper is listed as published on 2026-11-17, which falls just before this window. The previous fetch missed it, so it is recorded here to catch up. Details come from search snippets and the preprint, not the full text.
