## Papers Found (2026-09-07)

### 1. Metagenome-scale modeling to assess microbiome metabolic complementarity for precision microbiota transplantation therapies
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Clinical outcomes of fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) remain unpredictable because donor-recipient compatibility is not systematically assessed. Zhang, Holton, Ferrer, Tripp, Richter, Dixit, and Urtecho apply metagenome-scale metabolic modeling (community flux-balance-style simulation) to donor and recipient shotgun metagenomes to quantify metabolic niche complementarity — the degree to which a donor community's predicted resource-utilization profile fills gaps in a recipient's depleted microbiome. The model's complementarity scores are used to predict post-FMT community composition and engraftment success. The approach offers a computational framework for principled donor selection in FMT and related microbiota transplantation therapies, moving beyond current largely empirical donor-matching practice.
- **Link:** [10.1080/19490976.2026.2725403](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2026.2725403)

### 2. Protease-serpin-microbiome interactions in inflammatory bowel disease: toward integrated biomarkers of gut health
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Almalki, De Pierri, Méric, Mkaouar, Slimani, Mariaule, Tebassi, Akermi, Maguin, Hernandez, Raittz, Rhimi, and Asnicar review evidence that proteolytic imbalance — excess host and microbial serine protease activity relative to their endogenous inhibitors (serpins) — drives intestinal epithelial barrier disruption and sustains chronic inflammation in IBD. The review synthesizes host-serpin and microbiome-encoded serpin/protease-inhibitor data (building on prior work identifying microbiota-derived serine protease inhibitors such as siropins) and proposes an integrated protease-serpin-microbiome axis as a candidate biomarker panel for gut barrier health, with implications for stratifying IBD patients and monitoring disease activity or treatment response.
- **Link:** [10.1080/19490976.2026.2726641](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2026.2726641)

### 3. Bacteroides cellulosilyticus-derived 2-hydroxyphenylacetic acid rectifies hepatic lipid homeostasis in MASLD by targeting the PPARγ-CD36 axis
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Chen et al. identify the gut commensal *Bacteroides cellulosilyticus* as a producer of 2-hydroxyphenylacetic acid (2HPAA), a microbial metabolite that reaches the liver via enterohepatic circulation. Using single-cell sequencing and lipidomic analysis in a MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) model, the authors show 2HPAA inhibits hepatic PPARγ signaling, suppressing expression of the fatty-acid transporter CD36 and inhibiting CD36 palmitoylation while enhancing palmitate conversion efficiency in hepatocytes. Net effect is reduced hepatic lipid accumulation and attenuated MASLD progression, nominating the *B. cellulosilyticus*-2HPAA-PPARγ-CD36 axis as a candidate therapeutic target for fatty liver disease via gut-liver axis metabolite signaling.
- **Link:** DOI unresolved this cycle — [PubMed 42677827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42677827/) / [PMC13540161](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13540161/)
