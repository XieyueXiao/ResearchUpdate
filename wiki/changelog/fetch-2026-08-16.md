## Papers Found (2026-08-16)

### 1. Systematic profiling of growth interactions in human gut microbiome species
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Microbial interactions shape the composition and stability of the human gut microbiome, but the underlying mechanisms of species-species growth interactions have rarely been systematically profiled. Buyanbadrakh et al. used pairwise spent-medium growth assays across 36 representative human gut bacterial strains and found that interactions are predominantly inhibitory, largely explained by resource depletion or medium acidification. For the minority of growth-promoting interactions, the authors identify two distinct mechanisms: Clostridium perfringens promotes growth of Mediterraneibacter gnavus via extracellular vesicles, while Veillonella parvula raises environmental pH (an effect enhanced by guanine supplementation) to enable growth of the acid-sensitive species Parabacteroides merdae, with the pH-buffering effect persisting in multi-species communities containing diverse acid-producing taxa. The work offers a systematic mechanistic map of a mostly-competitive gut interaction network, relevant to community assembly and colonization-resistance models.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76526-z) (preprint: [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.19.693361v1))

### 2. Gut microbiota restricts intestinal lipid uptake via triggering Myd88 signalling and the degradation of phosphatidylcholine in bile
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology (IF ~28)
- **Highlights:** Using gnotobiotic mouse models and stable isotope-labelled lipid tracers combined with lipidomics and proteomics, this study shows host dietary lipid uptake depends on microbial colonization status: microbiota-colonized mice retained up to 12-fold more lipid in gut contents than germ-free animals, indicating the microbiota actively restricts intestinal fat absorption rather than merely correlating with it. Mechanistically, gut microbes trigger Myd88 signalling that downregulates hepatic Cyp7b1 activity, increasing taurocholate production; taurocholate in turn stimulates biliary phospholipase A1 activity, degrading phosphatidylcholine needed for luminal micelle formation and thereby limiting lipid uptake. The findings add a bile-lipid-metabolism axis to the gut microbiota's known roles in host energy harvest and metabolic regulation, with potential relevance to obesity/metabolic syndrome mechanisms.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02434-z)
