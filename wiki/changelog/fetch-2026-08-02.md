## Papers Found (2026-08-02)

### 1. Microbiota-derived 10-hydroxystearic acid activates PPARα to restore gut epithelial barrier integrity and enhance anti-retroviral therapy
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology (IF ~28)
- **Highlights:** UC Davis researchers (Kramer et al.) identify 10-hydroxystearic acid (10-HSA), a fatty-acid metabolite produced by the probiotic gut bacterium *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, as a driver of intestinal epithelial repair. Using X-ray crystallography and transcriptomics, the team show 10-HSA directly binds the nuclear receptor PPARα, inducing lipid metabolism, mitochondrial regeneration, and epigenetic histone crotonylation that together promote gut epithelial renewal. In two independent non-human primate studies of SIV/HIV infection at the UC Davis National Primate Research Center, 10-HSA alone improved gut barrier integrity, reduced inflammatory signaling, and partially restored gut microbiota composition; combined with antiretroviral therapy (ART), it produced faster clearance of viral burden than ART alone — positioning a microbiota-derived metabolite as a candidate adjunct therapy for HIV-associated gut dysfunction.
- **Link:** [doi:10.1038/s41564-026-02433-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02433-0)
