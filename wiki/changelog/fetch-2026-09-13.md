## Papers Found (2026-09-13)

### 1. Defined human Clostridia consortia reverse colitis via dual effects of tryptophan metabolites on microbiota and immunity
- **Journal:** Cell Host & Microbe (IF ~30)
- **Highlights:** Oka, Bongers, Mishima et al. developed two live biotherapeutic products (LBPs) — defined human Clostridia consortia termed "17-mix" and "11-mix" — and tested their therapeutic efficacy in established colitis using murine models, including a T-cell-mediated chronic colitis model and a pathobiont-driven gnotobiotic colitis model. Both LBPs reversed established disease through a dual mechanism: direct inhibition of resident colitogenic pathobionts, and IL-10-independent activation of the host anti-inflammatory aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathway via bacterial tryptophan catabolites. The work positions defined Clostridia consortia as a candidate live-biotherapeutic strategy for IBD that acts simultaneously on microbiota composition and host immune signaling, distinguishing it from single-strain or IL-10-dependent probiotic approaches.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2026.08.003)
