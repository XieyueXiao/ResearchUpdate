### 1. Microbial-derived tryptophan metabolites in the infant gut associate with increased risk of asthma following cesarean section
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Cesarean section (CS) is linked to a modestly higher risk of childhood asthma, but the microbial and metabolic mechanism is unclear. Jiang, Dai et al. used the prospective Canadian CHILD birth cohort to build CS-associated gut microbial scores from infant stool and relate them to the faecal metabolome. They tested these scores against asthma diagnosed at age 5. The 1-year CS microbial scores were associated with higher asthma risk at age 5 and with altered faecal metabolomes, marked especially by elevated microbial tryptophan metabolites. These metabolites statistically mediated part of the CS–asthma association. This points to early-life tryptophan metabolism, plausibly through indole/AhR signalling, as a link between delivery mode and immune development. **Possible tension:** a COPSAC study of newborn *blood* found CS associated with *lower* tryptophan metabolites. The sample type and age differ (newborn blood vs 1-year stool), so the two results may not truly conflict. The study is observational. Listed as Nat Commun vol. 17, article 10513, with an online date of about 2026-11-20/21 (exact date unconfirmed).
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77355-w)

### Off-window / off-list notes
- **Scientific Reports (not a target journal), 2026-11-20:** Park, Koh, Lee et al., "Gut microbial enrichment of *Blautia* and elevated serum butyrate in IgA nephropathy". This is a multicentre cross-sectional study at six hospitals with more than 280 participants. IgAN patients showed *Blautia* and *Ruminococcus gnavus* group enrichment and higher serum butyrate (P = 0.017) and lactate (P < 0.001). *Blautia* correlated with blood pressure, creatinine and proteinuria, and the result was replicated in a second cohort (P = 0.008). The elevated serum butyrate contrasts with reports of reduced faecal SCFAs in IgAN. One aggregator listed it under *Microbiome*, but Nature's listing gives *Scientific Reports*.
