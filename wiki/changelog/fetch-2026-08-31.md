## Papers Found (2026-08-31)

### 1. Complete genome-derived metabolic interactions reveal the impact of gut ecology on human health
- **Journal:** Cell Reports (IF ~8)
- **Highlights:** Gu et al. construct genome-scale metabolic models from 1,150 complete (closed) gut bacterial genomes rather than draft assemblies, showing that draft-genome fragmentation systematically omits transport-protein functions and thereby distorts predicted cross-feeding networks. Using the complete-genome models, the authors show that metabolic interaction asymmetry — not random co-occurrence — stratifies gut strains into four ecological roles ("active players," "resource predators," "resource utilizers," and "resource contributors"), each with distinct signatures of metabolite exchange, competitive exclusion, and secondary-metabolite production, and that these roles trace back to genomic traits and niche specialization. The work argues for complete-genome (vs. draft-assembly) references as a methodological requirement for accurate community-scale metabolic modeling of the gut microbiome.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117913)
