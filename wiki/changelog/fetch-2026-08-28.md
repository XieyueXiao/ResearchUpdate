## Papers Found (2026-08-28)

### 1. Mouth-to-gut microbial transmission signatures enable robust, non-invasive diagnosis of gastrointestinal cancers
- **Journal:** Cell Host & Microbe (IF ~30)
- **Highlights:** The oral cavity and gut are connected via continuous microbial seeding, but whether this "leakage" has diagnostic value for gastrointestinal disease has been unclear. Jang, Huh, and colleagues (lead contact Jihyun F. Kim, Yonsei University) profiled paired oral and fecal metagenomes from 507 participants and built a quantitative "mouth-to-feces" (MF) transmission index capturing the abundance of oral-origin taxa detected in stool. MF transmission was significantly elevated in patients with gastric and colorectal cancer relative to healthy controls, and diagnostic classifiers built on MF signatures outperformed standard fecal occult blood testing (FOBT) for both cancer types. Elevated oral-to-gut transmission also correlated with markers of intestinal barrier disruption and inflammation, suggesting a mechanistic link (barrier breakdown permitting oral colonizers to establish in the gut) rather than a purely incidental association. Published online 20 August 2026; DOI confirmed via search snippets (direct publisher/Crossref fetch blocked, see Notes) but not independently verified against the primary PDF.
- **Link:** [DOI: 10.1016/j.chom.2026.07.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2026.07.007)
