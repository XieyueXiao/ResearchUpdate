### 1. Food group intake during childhood and adolescence and risk of IBD: a pooled study of three Scandinavian birth cohorts
- **Journal:** Gut (IF ~24)
- **Highlights:** The question was whether early-life diet shapes later inflammatory bowel disease risk. Earlier ABIS/MoBa work (Guo et al., *Gut* 2024) looked only at ages 1 and 3, so this study extends the window to adolescence. The authors pooled food-group intake data from three Scandinavian birth cohorts, 111,999 participants in total, and took IBD diagnoses from national patient registers through 2024. High versus low salty-snack intake in adolescence (chips and popcorn) was associated with about 32% higher risk of any IBD and about 51% higher risk of ulcerative colitis. Higher fruit intake in childhood and adolescence, and childhood fish intake, were associated with lower Crohn's disease risk. The salty-snack association weakened after further adjustment. The study is observational, and press coverage reports no microbiome measurements. Gut-microbiome or barrier effects are only a hypothesised mechanism. Press coverage appeared on 2026-11-24 (Scimex, MedicalXpress, News-Medical).
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2026-339600) · [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-11-childhood-diet-high-fruit-fish.html) · [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/lots-of-salty-snacks-in-your-teens-linked-to-inflammatory-bowel-disease)
