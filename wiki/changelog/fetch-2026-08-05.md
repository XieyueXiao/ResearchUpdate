## Papers Found (2026-08-05)

### 1. Effect of a complementary feeding intervention based on iron- and zinc-biofortified pearl millet on the gut microbiota in 12–18-month-old children: a randomized trial
- **Journal:** Nature Communications (IF ~17)
- **Highlights:** Iron and zinc deficiency in early childhood is often addressed with high-dose supplements that can disrupt gut microbiota and cause GI side effects; biofortified staple crops have been proposed as a gentler alternative but their microbiome effects were untested. In a nine-month randomized trial of 223 children aged 12–18 months in urban Mumbai slums, half received complementary foods made with pearl millet biofortified to 8.7 mg iron/100 g (vs. 3 mg/100 g standard millet), with stool samples and shotgun/16S-based microbiota profiling collected at baseline and trial end. Children in the biofortified-millet group showed favorable shifts in gut microbial community structure, including increased functional pathway activity linked to pathogen defense and antioxidant metabolism and reduced markers associated with harmful bacteria, without the adverse GI effects typical of high-dose iron supplementation. The findings position biofortified staple crops as a sustainable, food-based, microbiome-friendly strategy for correcting micronutrient deficiency in young children, complementing (or substituting for) pharmacological supplementation.
- **Link:** [10.1038/s41467-026-75674-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75674-6)
