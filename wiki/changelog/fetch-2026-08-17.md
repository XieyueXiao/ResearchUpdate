## Papers Found (2026-08-17)

### 1. Maternal influences on infant gut microbiome and health
- **Journal:** Nature (IF ~64)
- **Highlights:** Longitudinal multi-omics study metagenomically sequencing 4,526 faecal samples from 714 mother–infant pairs in the Dutch Lifelines NEXT birth cohort, spanning 12 weeks of pregnancy to 1 year postpartum, integrated with 474 clinical/exposure variables and ultra-deep sequencing of breast milk and vaginal microbiomes. The maternal gut microbiome undergoes only subtle compositional change across pregnancy and postpartum, shaped by diet, infections, and pre-pregnancy smoking. It is the dominant reservoir for infant gut bacterial strains — median strain-sharing between mother and infant was ~65% at two weeks to one month, falling to ~25% by 12 months — with only occasional strain contributions from vaginal or breast-milk microbiomes; higher maternal species abundance increased the odds of strain transmission. Delivery mode and feeding mode were the strongest drivers of infant gut microbiome development overall, but maternal gut microbiome composition and specific taxon levels independently predicted infant eczema risk in the first year of life, with maternal alpha diversity remaining significant after adjusting for maternal smoking history and family allergic disease history.
- **Link:** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10922-9)

Publication date resolves to 2026-08-13, three days outside a strict 24h window, but this is the first cycle the paper surfaced in searches and it was not previously logged — included given its scale and direct relevance (mother-infant strain transmission, eczema prediction). ### 2. Synthetic microbial communities: emerging live biotherapeutics for targeted gut microbiome modulation
- **Journal:** Gut Microbes (IF ~12)
- **Highlights:** Open-access review by Ru Zhang et al. surveying synthetic microbial communities (SynComs) as a new generation of live biotherapeutic products (LBPs) for targeted gut microbiome modulation, covering community design/assembly strategies, use of SynComs to dissect host–microbiome mechanistic questions, and their clinical translation path alongside FDA-approved LBPs (e.g., RBX2660/live-jslm, VE303, SER-109) for indications such as recurrent C. difficile infection.
